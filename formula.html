Start here: how Excel formulas work
  • Every formula starts with =. Without it, Excel just shows what you typed.
  • A cell address is column letter + row number. B3 means column B, row 3.
  • A range is first cell, colon, last cell. D2:D6 means D2, D3, D4, D5 and D6.
  • Math symbols: + add, - subtract, * multiply, / divide, ^ power. Excel does * and / before + and −, unless you use parentheses.
  • Functions do bigger jobs. Type the name, then parentheses with the inputs inside: =SUM(D2:D6). Separate inputs with commas: =ROUND(C2,2).
  • Words inside a formula go in quotes: =IF(D2>100,"High","Low"). Numbers don't.
  • Point to cells instead of typing numbers. =B2*C2, not =12*4, so the answer updates when the data changes.

Error values

ErrorUsually means
#DIV/0!Dividing by zero or a blank cell, or AVERAGEIF found no matches.
#N/AA lookup couldn't find the value. Check spelling and the exact-match argument.
#REF!The formula points to a cell that doesn't exist, like a column number past the edge of a VLOOKUP table, or a deleted cell.
#NAME?Excel doesn't recognize a name: a misspelled function, or text missing its quotes.
#VALUE!Math on text, or the wrong kind of argument.
