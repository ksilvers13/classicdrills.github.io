Reference sheet (tax year 2026)

Filing status, in the order to test

  • Marital status is set on December 31. Divorced or legally separated by then means unmarried for the whole year.
  • Married filing jointly: married on Dec 31, or your spouse died during the year.
  • Qualifying surviving spouse: spouse died in either of the two prior years (2024 or 2025 for a 2026 return), you haven't remarried, a dependent child lived with you all year, and you paid over half the cost of the home.
  • Head of household: unmarried or "considered unmarried" (spouse didn't live in the home the last 6 months of the year), paid over half the cost of keeping up a home, and a qualifying person lived there over half the year. A dependent parent doesn't have to live with you.
  • Married filing separately: married, not considered unmarried, and filing apart from the spouse.
  • Single: unmarried with no qualifying person.

Standard deduction

  • Age 65 and blindness are separate conditions. Someone who is both gets two add-ons.
  • A dependent's standard deduction is the greater of $1,350 or earned income + $450, capped at the regular amount ($16,100 single).
  • If one spouse filing separately itemizes, the other's standard deduction is $0.
  • The $6,000 senior deduction (2025 through 2028) is separate from the standard deduction. It phases out above $75,000 MAGI ($150,000 joint) and isn't available to married filing separately.

Form 1040 flow

  • Total income (wages, interest, unemployment…) − adjustments = AGI
  • AGI − standard or itemized deduction = taxable income (never below 0)
  • Tax on taxable income − credits = total tax (never below 0 for nonrefundable credits)
  • Payments (W-2 box 2 withholding) − total tax = refund if positive, amount owed if negative
